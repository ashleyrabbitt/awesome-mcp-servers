## Monetize the existing traffic

Primary path: useful article or directory listing → free Launch Lab tool → completed brief or comparison → $350 AI Working Session → scoped workflow setup from $1,500.

Maker path: free submission → $497 Founder Launch Kit or approved $49 / seven-day sponsored placement. Keep sponsored positions labeled and separate from editorial judgment. Quote newsletter sponsorship only after sharing actual audience evidence.

Existing free courses remain free. Do not enable checkout for an unfinished video product. Use approved partner links only when relevant to the reader's task, with the existing disclosure and program-specific tracking. No assumed affiliate approval, payout or ROI.

Establish the baseline from connected analytics and payment reports. Do not store private account totals in this public repository. Sessions are not unique people, outbound clicks may be undercounted, and portfolio invoices are not site-attributed revenue without supporting records.

At this traffic level, test a service conversion path before depending on display advertising. Planning scenario, not forecast: two working sessions ($700) plus one $1,500 setup = $2,200 gross before fees, tax and delivery costs. Count session-to-setup customers once per distinct paid deliverable; don't silently double-count credited fees.

## Thirty-day experiment

Days 1–7: verify release, make one useful demo, add contextual links from the strongest related pages, and record baseline eligible sessions. Use the existing Power Notes consent flow. Do not auto-enroll tool users.

Days 8–14: publish three short demonstrations, each showing a completed artifact and linking to the matching free tool. Use a single working-session CTA. Record qualified inquiries and why they did or did not book.

Days 15–21: deliver the first agreed session, record preparation/delivery time and direct costs, request permission before using client results in a case study, and offer implementation only when the task justifies it.

Days 22–30: compare traffic, tool use, offer clicks, submitted inquiries, booked sessions, paid invoices, refunds and contribution after delivery costs. If views do not become tool use, improve the entry page. If tool use does not become inquiries, test the scope and CTA. If inquiries do not become sales, investigate fit and scheduling before building another feature.

New events: launch_tool_action (plan_built, plan_downloaded) and launch_offer_click (tool, session, setup, maker). Events contain fixed labels and page path, never entered plan text. Browser analytics can be blocked; missing events are not zero activity. CTA clicks are not leads and leads are not payments. Existing service requests and payment records remain authoritative. Use request/invoice references in a private revenue log; never put customer data in the public repository.

Weekly scorecard columns: week, source page, eligible sessions, plan builds, downloads, offer clicks, qualified inquiries, bookings, paid invoices, gross collected, refunds, payment fees, delivery hours, direct costs, attributed source, next change. Leave unknown values blank.

## Recorded training: start with three short demos

Screen recordings are enough; a face camera is optional. Make one take with a short introduction, a real walkthrough and a clear next step. Add accurate captions and a text transcript. Use fictional data and hide notifications. Do not claim savings or sales without actual measured evidence.

### Video 1 — Give your first agent a clear job (6–8 minutes)

Opening: “Before connecting an agent to your accounts, give it one job you can check. In this lesson, we'll build a weekly research brief plan with a reviewer and a stopping point.”

0:00–0:40: show the finished Markdown brief. 0:40–3:30: open Agent Launch Planner, load the fictional sample, explain the eight fields and replace the outcome with a familiar task. 3:30–5:30: build the plan, show duplicate-trigger and failure tests, explain that they are not yet executed. 5:30–6:30: download the file and identify the next implementation step.

Close: “Use the free planner for your own task. If you'd like help making it work in your business, bring your brief to a $350 working session. We'll agree the scope first.”

### Video 2 — Choose an AI tool with one fair comparison (5–7 minutes)

Opening: “A long tool list doesn't tell you what will help you. Let's give two tools the same small job and count the review work.”

Show Tool Trial Lab; choose two candidates; enter one permitted fictional brief; explain the expected answer and scoring criteria. Demonstrate where actual results and correction time belong. If outputs have not actually been tested, leave scores empty and explicitly say so. Export the comparison framework. Link the free comparison tool and the same working-session offer.

### Video 3 — Keep a quote from disappearing into your inbox (5–7 minutes)

Opening: “This free pilot gives you a small review queue for quotes. It helps you decide what to follow up on; it doesn't send messages for you.”

Open Quote Follow-up Desk, load fictional sample records, select a quote, explain due date and suppression, mark a manual next step and export a backup. Show that local storage is not a shared CRM. Explain which business-specific integration would need a scoped setup. CTA: “If you need this connected to your actual lead source and booking process, ask about a scoped Lead Recovery Pilot.”

## When to offer paid recordings

After the free demos establish repeated demand, test a separate $49–$99 workshop bundle with completed recordings, editable templates and clear support terms. Price is a proposal, not a live offer. Keep existing promised free courses free. A $350 guided session and team training from $1,500 are already clearer near-term offers than an unfinished course subscription.

Record lesson completion and qualified inquiries, not just video views. Do not create another training brand: AI Superpower hosts discovery and free demos; AI Teachers hosts teaching services; Agency handles implementation; Wealth Experiment can show a documented business case when there is genuine evidence.
